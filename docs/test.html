Sennett knows his craft. The Craftsman is an essay about the desire to do a job well as an end in itself, and this definition of craftsmanship, with which Sennett begins his book, has in a certain sense been a leitmotif throughout the writing career of this philosopher of society. The result is a book that's hard to put down.

Sennett's considerations begin with his recalling a passing conversation with his teacher Hannah Arendt in the wake of the 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis. Arendt insisted on her standpoint — developed in The Human Condition — that the engineer, or more generally, the active person is not the master of his own fate.
